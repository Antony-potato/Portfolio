## Context

**Koa Towers** is a vertical residential development by Onix Living in Cancún. As with Aldea Savia, apartment availability was shared through PDFs and spreadsheets that quickly went stale. I built the project's availability site starting from the architecture I had already built for Aldea Savia, adapting it to multi-level towers.

## The Challenge

- **From lots to towers:** instead of lots on a plot, apartments had to be grouped by tower and by level, each level with its own floor plan.
- **Show the towers from several angles** without loading a heavy 3D model in the browser.
- **Keep inventory editable from a spreadsheet**, just like Aldea Savia.

## What I did

- **3D-style viewer between views:** the towers are explored through a drag-controlled `<canvas>` frame sequence, with video transitions between views and hotspots to enter each tower. It feels like a 3D tour without loading a 3D model.
- **Interactive per-level floor plans:** a modal with each level's SVG plan (pan and zoom), a level selector, and a synced apartment list and detail view. Hovering the plan highlights the apartment in the list, and vice versa.
- **Inventory from Google Sheets:** I reused Aldea Savia's data service (published CSV, 5-minute cache) and extended it to group units by level and attach each level's plan from S3.
- **Scroll-scrub hero** on canvas, as in Aldea Savia.
- **Supporting pages:** location with Google Maps, a media gallery, a brochure viewer and a per-apartment contact form.
- **Astro 6 + native Web Components + CSS Custom Properties**, with styles split by module (states, controls, panel, list, responsive).

## Outcome

- Inventory is managed from a spreadsheet and the site updates without a redeploy.
- Reusing Aldea Savia's architecture let me focus on what was new: the multi-view viewer and per-level plans.
